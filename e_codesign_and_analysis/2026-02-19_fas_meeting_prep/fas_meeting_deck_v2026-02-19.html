Overview

What is the Nuclear Winter Data Commons?

The Problem

Nuclear winter climate model outputs are large, complex, and inaccessible to non-specialist researchers and policymakers. The raw data lives in netCDF files and specialized simulation archives — not a spreadsheet.

The Solution

NWDC standardizes and simplifies those outputs into clean, spreadsheet-ready datasets — low barrier to entry for any researcher, analyst, or journalist who wants to ask country-level questions.

The Goal

Enable interdisciplinary analysis linking nuclear policy, climate science, and humanitarian impacts — at the country level, across all plausible conflict scenarios.

Open access  ·  193 countries  ·  7 conflict scenarios  ·  9+ variables  ·  osf.io/e28gq

Progress

Progress Since January 2025

New Dataset — Starvation

Population starving by country and scenario, with sub-scenarios for international trade status, livestock feed diversion, and food waste reduction. Derived from Xia et al. 2022.

New Dataset — Downwelling Shortwave

Surface solar radiation reduction by country and scenario. Provides a direct measure of agricultural and ecosystem light limitation across all 7 soot injection levels.

Pipeline Improvements

Streamlined back-end for adding and updating datasets. Clean OSF versioning with automated duplicate removal. Standardized CSV/JSON outputs for consistent downstream use.

Publication

Paper accepted for publication (was a preprint at our January 2025 meeting). Revisions due March 1, 2026. Country report dashboard built as companion tool.

Vision

NWDC as a Platform

🔎

Research Enablement

Lower the barrier for economists, public health researchers, security analysts, and policymakers to engage with nuclear winter science — without needing to run Earth System Models themselves.

📍

Policy Translation

Translate climate model outputs into country-level impact metrics that map directly onto policy questions: food security, refugee flows, economic disruption, and regional stability.

☠

Nuclear Risk Reduction

By making the consequences of nuclear conflict quantifiable at the country level, NWDC supports the evidence base for arms control, disarmament advocacy, and risk communication.
